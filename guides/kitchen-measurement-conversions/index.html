How to measure more accurately

Use the right measuring tool

For water, milk and other liquids, use a liquid measuring cup on a level surface. For flour and sugar, use dry measuring cups or a kitchen scale.

Don't confuse fluid ounces with ounces

Fluid ounces measure volume; ounces measure mass. Eight fluid ounces of an ingredient do not necessarily weigh eight ounces.

For baking, weigh ingredients

When a recipe provides gram measurements, a kitchen scale avoids differences caused by scooping, packing or settling ingredients.

Check which cup standard a recipe uses

These charts use U.S. customary measurements. A metric cup is often 250 mL, while one U.S. customary cup is approximately 237 mL (usually rounded to 240 mL for cooking).

Frequently asked questions

How many tablespoons are in a cup?

One U.S. cup contains 16 tablespoons. Each tablespoon equals 3 teaspoons, so one cup also equals 48 teaspoons.

How many milliliters are in a cup?

One U.S. customary cup is approximately 237 mL. For everyday recipe conversions it is commonly rounded to 240 mL. Some metric recipes use a 250 mL cup instead.

How many grams are in a cup?

There is no single answer: grams measure weight while cups measure volume. A cup of flour weighs much less than a cup of sugar. Use an ingredient-specific conversion or a scale.

Is a tablespoon the same everywhere?

No. These tables use the common U.S. cooking tablespoon of about 15 mL. Other countries may use different tablespoon sizes.

Reference: U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), Metric Kitchen. Values are rounded for practical kitchen use.
