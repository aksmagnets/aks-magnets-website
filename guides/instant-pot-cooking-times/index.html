Quick release vs. natural release

Quick release

Carefully release steam following the appliance instructions once pressure cooking finishes. Useful when you want to stop cooking tender vegetables promptly. Keep hands and face away from escaping steam.

Natural release

Let the cooker depressurize on its own. Instant Pot recommends this approach for many starchy or high-liquid foods, such as beans, porridge and soup, to reduce splattering.

Pressure-cooking tips

Allow time to build pressure

The countdown begins only after the cooker reaches pressure. Depending on the food and volume, this can add considerable time.

Respect fill limits

Do not exceed the pressure-cooking maximum fill line. Foods that expand, such as rice and dried beans, require a lower fill limit. Check your model manual.

Add enough thin liquid

Pressure cooking requires liquid to generate steam. Requirements vary by cooker size and model; follow the manual and recipe rather than guessing.

Check food for doneness

Size, thickness and starting temperature change the results. Check safe internal temperatures for meat and poultry before serving.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a 5-minute recipe take much longer?

The pressure-cooking timer does not include preheating and pressure release. Both may add substantial time.

Can I open the lid as soon as the timer ends?

No. The lid must stay locked until the cooker has depressurized according to its instructions. Never force it open.

Does quick release work for beans or oatmeal?

Natural release is generally recommended for starchy or high-liquid foods because quick venting can cause foam or food to splatter through the valve.

What if I have a different Instant Pot model?

Consult its instructions and approved recipes. Cooking programs, pressure levels and liquid requirements can vary.
