Using the vegan protein chart

Compare similar servings

One ounce of nuts and a cup of beans are very different portions. Review the serving-size column before comparing their protein values.

Look beyond the protein figure

Beans and grains also provide carbohydrates, while nuts and seeds often contain more fat and calories. Choose foods that suit your overall nutrition goals.

Use variety throughout the day

Legumes, soy foods, grains, nuts and seeds can all contribute protein to a varied vegan diet. You don't need to rely on one ingredient.

Read packaged-food labels

Protein powders, milk alternatives and meat substitutes vary considerably between products. Their Nutrition Facts labels are more reliable for the exact product you're eating.

Frequently asked questions

Which foods on this chart have the most protein per listed serving?

Pea protein powder (22 g per ¼ cup), brown rice protein powder (20 g per ¼ cup), soy protein powder (18 g per ¼ cup), and seitan (18 g per 3 oz) are among the highest entries. These servings are not directly comparable by weight.

Are beans good sources of vegan protein?

Yes. The chart includes lentils, soybeans, chickpeas and several other legumes. Their protein amounts depend on the type and serving size.

Do starred foods use cooked portions?

Yes. The asterisk is carried over from the magnet and means the stated serving is measured cooked.

Are these exact values for every brand?

No. Products and preparation methods vary. These are approximate reference values, not substitutes for a food package's nutrition label or an up-to-date food database.
