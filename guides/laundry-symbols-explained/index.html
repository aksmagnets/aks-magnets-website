Confused by the little symbols on clothing tags? Use this illustrated reference to understand common washing, bleaching, drying, ironing, and professional-cleaning instructions — so you can care for your clothes with confidence.

✓ 7 care categories✓ 35 illustrated symbols✓ Clear explanations
Start here: The five basic symbol shapes are a wash tub (washing), triangle (bleaching), square (drying), iron (ironing), and circle (professional cleaning). Dots usually indicate temperature, bars mean gentler treatment, and a cross means the marked process is not allowed. These are simplified educational illustrations; always follow the care label on your garment.

Helpful laundry care tips

Read the whole label

A garment may allow washing but prohibit tumble drying or ironing. Check every care symbol before you begin.

Treat temperatures as limits

A tub marked 40°C means the wash temperature should not exceed 40°C. It does not mean every garment needs hot water.

Pay attention to bars

One or two bars underneath a symbol call for progressively gentler handling — especially important for delicate items.

When in doubt, be gentle

Check the garment's written instructions and manufacturer guidance, especially for decorated, lined, or delicate garments.

Frequently asked questions

What does a crossed-out wash tub mean?

It means do not wash the garment. Check the professional-cleaning instructions or ask a textile-care professional.

What does a triangle with two lines mean?

Only non-chlorine (oxygen) bleach may be used when needed.

What does a square with a circle inside mean?

It is a tumble-drying symbol. Dots indicate dryer heat; the precise number of heat levels depends on the care-label system. A cross means do not tumble dry.

What do the dots on an iron mean?

One dot is low heat, two dots medium heat, and three dots high heat. The care label takes precedence over assumptions about fabric type.

What do P, F, and W inside a circle mean?

These letters specify permitted professional-cleaning methods. P and F indicate different dry-cleaning solvents, while W refers to professional wet cleaning.

Reference information: GINETEX care-symbol explanations (ISO 3758:2023) and Canada's Office of Consumer Affairs guide. The illustrations are simplified for quick recognition, not official licensed symbol artwork.
