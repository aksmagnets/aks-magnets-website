Laundry Symbols Explained

Confused by the little symbols on clothing tags? Use this illustrated reference to understand common washing, bleaching, drying, ironing, and professional-cleaning instructions — so you can care for your clothes with confidence.

✓ 5 care categories✓ Illustrated symbols✓ Clear explanations
Start here: The five basic shapes are a wash tub (washing), triangle (bleaching), square (drying), iron (ironing), and circle (professional cleaning). Dots usually indicate temperature, bars mean gentler treatment, and a cross means the marked process is not allowed. These are simplified educational illustrations; always follow the care label on your garment.

Washing symbols

↑ Categories

The wash tub tells you whether and how an item can be washed. Numbers indicate the maximum water temperature in °C; bars beneath the tub call for gentler handling.

Machine wash at 30°C

Use a wash cycle at or below 30°C (86°F).

Machine wash at 40°C

Use a wash cycle at or below 40°C (104°F).

Machine wash at 60°C

Use a wash cycle at or below 60°C (140°F), if the label allows.

Gentle cycle

One bar beneath the tub means reduced mechanical action, such as a permanent-press or gentle program.

Very gentle cycle

Two bars mean an especially mild process, often used for delicate fabrics.

Hand wash

Wash by hand as directed; avoid aggressive wringing.

Do not wash

Do not machine-wash or hand-wash. Check the other care instructions.

Bleaching symbols

↑ Categories

Triangles tell you whether bleach can be used. A crossed triangle means no bleaching; two diagonal lines limit you to non-chlorine bleach.

Bleaching allowed

An empty triangle means chlorine or oxygen bleach may be used when needed.

Non-chlorine bleach only

Use oxygen/non-chlorine bleach only, not chlorine bleach.

Do not bleach

Avoid all bleaching products.

Drying symbols

↑ Categories

A square with a circle refers to a tumble dryer. A plain square with lines inside refers to natural drying methods.

Tumble dry

Tumble drying is allowed; follow any temperature dots or other modifiers on the label.

Tumble dry low

One dot means low heat.

Tumble dry normal

Two dots mean the normal tumble-drying temperature setting.

Do not tumble dry

Avoid a tumble dryer; use an allowed natural-drying method.

Line dry

Hang the item to dry after removing excess water.

Dry flat

Lay the item flat to help it keep its shape.

Drip dry

Hang the item to dry while still wet; do not spin or wring unless the care label permits.

Dry in shade

Keep the garment out of direct sunlight while line drying.

Ironing symbols

↑ Categories

An iron symbol indicates whether ironing is allowed. Dots show the maximum heat level; a crossed iron means no ironing.

Iron on low

One dot: use a low-temperature setting suitable for the garment.

Iron on medium

Two dots: use a medium-temperature setting.

Iron on high

Three dots: a high-temperature setting is permitted.

Do not iron

Do not iron or press the fabric.

Professional cleaning symbols

↑ Categories

Circles are instructions for professional textile cleaners. Letters specify approved processes or solvents; bars mean a milder treatment.

Professional dry clean (P)

A cleaner may use the solvents permitted by the P designation.

Professional dry clean (F)

This designation limits cleaning to specified hydrocarbon solvents.

Professional wet clean (W)

The garment may receive a professional wet-cleaning process.

Mild professional cleaning

A bar underneath the circle indicates restrictions requiring a gentler process.

Do not dry clean

Do not use professional dry-cleaning solvents; check whether professional wet cleaning is separately allowed.

Helpful laundry care tips

Read the whole label

A garment may allow washing but prohibit tumble drying or ironing. Check every care symbol before you begin.

Treat temperatures as limits

A tub marked 40°C means the wash temperature should not exceed 40°C. It does not mean every garment needs hot water.

Pay attention to bars

One or two bars underneath a symbol call for progressively gentler handling — especially important for delicate items.

When in doubt, be gentle

Check the garment's written instructions and manufacturer guidance, especially for decorated, lined, or delicate garments.

Frequently asked questions

What does a crossed-out wash tub mean?

It means do not wash the garment. Check the professional-cleaning instructions or ask a textile-care professional.

What does a triangle with two lines mean?

Only non-chlorine (oxygen) bleach may be used when needed.

What does a square with a circle inside mean?

It is a tumble-drying symbol. One dot indicates low heat, while two dots allow normal heat. A cross means do not tumble dry.

What do the dots on an iron mean?

One dot is low heat, two dots medium heat, and three dots high heat. The care label takes precedence over assumptions about fabric type.

What do P, F, and W inside a circle mean?

These letters specify permitted professional-cleaning methods. P and F indicate different dry-cleaning solvents, while W refers to professional wet cleaning.

Reference information: GINETEX care-symbol explanations (ISO 3758:2023) and Canada's Office of Consumer Affairs guide. The illustrations are simplified for quick recognition, not official licensed symbol artwork.
